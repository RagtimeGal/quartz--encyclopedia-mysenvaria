Azimuth, altitude, and distance (AAD) is the most commonly used coordinate system for [[Encyclopedia Mysenvaria/Science/Technologies/Measurement|measuring]] and describing the position of [[Encyclopedia Mysenvaria/Geography/Abstract Features/Heaven|heavenly]] objects. AAD is a three dimensional coordinate system, differentiated from [[Encyclopedia Mysenvaria/Geography/Abstract Features/Span & Reach|span & reach]] which is used to tell something's position in two dimensional space on the [[Encyclopedia Mysenvaria/Geography/Natural Features/Plane|Plane]]. Altitude, azimuth, and distance is used for a multitude of purposes but primarily by [[Encyclopedia Mysenvaria/Science/Branches/Astronomy|astronomers]] to describe the position of [[Encyclopedia Mysenvaria/Science/Phenomena/Stars|stars]].

The origin of AAD coordinates is typically the [[Encyclopedia Mysenvaria/Geography/Abstract Features/Central Pole|pole]], but it may be used to measure anything's relative position from any other thing. 
- Azimuth is a measure of the position's angular direction along the Plane's horizon relative to the [[Encyclopedia Mysenvaria/Geography/Abstract Features/Cardinal Directions|north]], it's expressed in degrees increasing starward. 
- Altitude is a measure of the position's angular direction upwards from the Plane's horizon, it's expressed in degrees increasing upward to the zenith at 90°. Altitude can be negative to represent positions below the horizon.
- Distance is a measure of how far from the point of origin the position is located. It is expressed in some unit which may vary depending on the context. 
A position in the coordinate system is represented in the format (Azimuth°, Altitude°, Distance).
