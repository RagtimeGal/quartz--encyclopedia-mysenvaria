Stacking birds are a domesticated species of small nestless bird known for their ability to balance eggs and other individuals on their backs. Wild individuals are native to small portions of the [[Encyclopedia Mysenvaria/Geography/Natural Features/Southern Continent|Old World]] where they are not hunted by predators due to their poisonous feathers and skin.

The bird's meat may be eaten by plucking it free of all feathers, removing the skin, and then coating the remaining meat in charcoal and cooking it. This removes nearly all of the toxins from the meat and makes it edible. This was not practiced often historically by native cultures. Instead, the birds were raised primarily for their eggs and ornamental feathers. They served as iconic parts of ritual culture and played important roles in festivals and religions.
