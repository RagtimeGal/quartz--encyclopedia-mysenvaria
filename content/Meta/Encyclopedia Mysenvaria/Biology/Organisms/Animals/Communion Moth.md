# Writer's Note
- You have this species of moth, which in the case of Mysenvar are probably fucking massive moths, that have a life cycle incredibly similar to that of a Mexican jumping bean moth. People have domesticated these moths (and the plant they parasitize).
- The larvae of these moths, which burrow into the seeds of the plant, are capable of long-distance communication. They communicate bad things, like drowning and burning, and when others receive the signal they hop instinctively in the hopes to get away from it (even if they aren't directly affected by it).
  - Essentially in wild individuals, you imagine that most of them are probably around the same tree, so when something happens to one it's best practice to try and get away for your own survival.
  - These larvae only communicate this information to their own siblings, too. So genetically unrelated individuals do not receive this signal.
  - Wild individuals are alone already capable of communicating over incredibly vast distances. Their mother provides each individual with just enough Limn to form a star. The stars they produce are too intricate to entirely translate, but some Astronomers have a general idea of how they work.
- People took advantage of this and domesticated them. Domesticated larvae do not hop when directly in danger, but send their siblings who are not in danger the signal to hop. To make them hop people will heat a needle in a fire and tap them with it, essentially communicating in a morse code-esque language.
  - They also hop in a few other scenarios, which makes proper transportation of them so as to not send off signals pretty important. If they get too hot or cold from ambient temperatures they may begin signaling. They also can't be exposed to water or they will begin signaling.
